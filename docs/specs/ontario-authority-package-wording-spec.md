# Ontario Authority Package Wording Specification for Vero Permit

*Draft only. Requires Ontario legal, municipal-process, and professional-practice review before production use.*

## Executive summary

Ontario should not inherit British Columbia or Vancouver authority-package language. Ontario’s current building-code framework is its own regime: as of 2026, Ontario’s Building Code is O. Reg. 163/24, which adopts the National Building Code of Canada 2020 as amended by Ontario’s amendment document, and Ontario’s permit system is enforced primarily by municipalities through chief building officials and inspectors rather than through BC-style assurance language. Ontario’s official permit vocabulary also differs materially from BC/Vancouver vocabulary: the core provincial form is **Application for a Permit to Construct or Demolish**, Ontario uses **Schedule 1: Designer Information** and **BCIN** concepts for many design scenarios, municipalities review for **Ontario Building Code**, **zoning**, and **applicable law**, and electrical work generally sits on a separate **ESA** track. citeturn0search0turn19search13turn24search9turn25search0turn25search1turn21search0turn10view7

For Vero, the safest Ontario-facing product position is **documentation support**, not compliance certification or professional assurance. The package should be framed as a **permit support package** or **submission support package** assembled to help an applicant, owner, builder, or retained professional organize materials for municipal or other principal-authority submission. It should never say or imply that Vero issues permits, determines code compliance, replaces the municipality, replaces the CBO or inspector, replaces the applicant’s designer, or replaces required architect/engineer/BCIN/ESA/external authority functions. citeturn24search4turn25search0turn25search1turn23view1turn10view7

The copy below is designed to stay conservative across Ontario while leaving room for later municipal overlays for Toronto, Ottawa, Mississauga, Hamilton, and other authorities. That matters because Ontario municipalities use different public-facing labels for zoning pre-checks, inspection stages, permit-closeout language, and occupancy processes. Toronto emphasizes final inspection to close the permit and has a **Zoning Applicable Law Certificate** product; Mississauga uses **occupancy permit** language for some new homes; Hamilton distinguishes **occupancy** from **final – completion of the building permit** and can require an occupancy application form; Ottawa has municipality-specific workflows and forms, including an **Agency Letter of Approval** process for certain cases that should not be generalized across Ontario. citeturn10view5turn18view3turn14view1turn15view1turn22search3turn16search5

## Package purpose and preferred naming

**Ontario authority package purpose**

The Ontario package should be described as a document set assembled **to support permit submission and review**, not as a legal or technical determination of compliance. That posture fits the provincial form structure, which is built around an application made by the owner or authorized agent, with supporting plans, specifications, schedules, and documents that enable the chief building official to determine whether the proposal contravenes applicable law. It also fits municipal review language, which consistently says the municipality reviews applications for compliance with the Ontario Building Code, zoning, and applicable law. citeturn21search0turn18view0turn18view2turn24search4

**Recommended package title options**

Preferred customer-facing options:

- **Ontario Permit Support Package**
- **Ontario Building Permit Submission Support Package**
- **Ontario Municipal Submission Support Package**
- **Ontario Authority Submission Support Package**

Recommended internal-only option:

- **Ontario Authority Package**

The word **support** is the key safety term. It accurately signals assistance with organizing materials without implying permit issuance, municipal acceptance, or professional assurance. The more direct phrase **building permit** is also safe when the package truly relates to a building-permit workflow, because that is the official Ontario term used by the province and municipalities. **Authority package** is usable, but it is better as an internal Vero label or as a secondary label because Ontario’s official public-facing vocabulary is more often “building permit,” “municipality,” “building department,” “chief building official,” or “principal authority.” citeturn24search4turn25search0turn25search1turn24search0

**Recommended short description**

Recommended default short description:

> Draft permit support package assembled to help organize materials for an Ontario building permit submission to the applicable municipality or other principal authority, subject to applicant review, required schedules, professional sign-off where required, and all municipal and applicable-law requirements.

That description aligns with the official provincial application structure, with municipal review language, and with the fact that some Ontario submissions may involve an upper-tier municipality, board of health, or conservation authority in addition to or instead of a lower-tier municipality for specific sewage/plumbing contexts. citeturn21search0turn24search0turn24search4turn25search1

**Recommended “prepared for submission” language**

Recommended default line:

> Prepared for submission support only. Final submission content, completeness, signatures, seals, schedules, and authority-specific requirements must be confirmed by the applicant and any retained qualified designer, architect, engineer, or other required professional before filing.

That wording is safer than “submission-ready,” “city-ready,” or “approved for submission.” Ontario’s official form puts responsibility on the applicant for truthfulness and completeness, and municipal/public sources make clear that review and permit issuance remain municipal or principal-authority functions. citeturn21search0turn24search4turn25search0turn25search1

## Disclaimer language and regulatory boundaries

**Recommended disclaimer language**

Recommended baseline disclaimer:

> This is a draft permit support package generated to help organize information for an Ontario building permit application. It does not issue a permit, does not determine Ontario Building Code compliance, does not confirm compliance with zoning or other applicable law, and does not replace review, acceptance, inspection, or decisions by the municipality, chief building official, inspector, or any other principal authority.

This language tracks Ontario’s actual review structure: municipalities and chief building officials review and issue permits, inspectors conduct mandatory inspections, and permit issuance depends on Building Code, zoning, and applicable-law review. citeturn25search0turn25search1turn18view0turn10view2

**Schedule C-B separation language**

Recommended explicit separation block:

> Ontario package only. Do not use BC Schedule C-B, Letters of Assurance, or other British Columbia or Vancouver assurance wording for Ontario projects unless a specific Ontario authority or regulated professional process expressly requires different wording.

This separation is necessary because Ontario’s official permit language is organized around the provincial permit form, Schedule 1 designer information, BCIN/qualified-designer concepts, and Ontario architect/engineer general-review rules—not BC Schedule C-B terminology. citeturn21search0turn23view1turn23view3turn23view2

**CBO / municipal inspector wording**

Recommended role wording:

> The applicable municipality or other principal authority reviews permit applications and issues permits. The chief building official coordinates and oversees Building Code enforcement within the jurisdiction, and building inspectors conduct required inspections and may issue compliance or stop-work orders where authorized.

This wording closely reflects Ontario’s published explanation of the CBO and inspector roles and should be preferred over vague “city reviewer” language when role clarity matters. citeturn25search0turn25search1turn24search3

**Schedule 1 / BCIN / designer wording**

Recommended wording:

> Where required, include completed **Schedule 1: Designer Information** for each individual who reviews and takes responsibility for design activities, together with the appropriate BCIN or other basis for exemption. Do not imply that Vero supplies a BCIN or acts as the designer of record.

This comes directly from Ontario’s permit-form structure. The provincial form instructs applicants to attach Schedule 1 for each individual who reviews and takes responsibility for design activities, and the form distinguishes BCIN-qualified designers, other designers, and exemption bases. It also states that Schedule 1 is not required to be completed by Ontario architects holding the relevant OAA status or by Ontario professional engineers holding the relevant PEO licence/authorization status. citeturn21search0turn19search0

**General review / architect / engineer wording**

Recommended wording:

> Where Ontario law requires design and general review by an architect, a professional engineer, or both, all required professional retention, signatures, seals, and general-review obligations remain with those licensed professionals. Vero may help organize supporting materials, but it does not perform regulated design or general review.

That language is consistent with the OAA/PEO joint bulletin and the standard owner commitment form, which treat general review as a professional obligation tied to licensed architects and/or professional engineers and linked to the permit-basis documents. The OAA and PEO also distinguish legally required Building Code general review from broader contract administration or non-code review services. citeturn9view1turn23view1turn23view0turn23view2turn23view3

**Applicable law / zoning / municipal precheck wording**

Recommended wording:

> Ontario permit review includes not only the Ontario Building Code but also zoning and other applicable law. This package may include placeholders or prompts for zoning review, applicable-law review, and municipality-specific pre-check materials, but authority-specific requirements must be confirmed with the applicable municipality or principal authority.

“Applicable law” is an official Ontario Building Code concept, and municipalities use it explicitly. Toronto uses **Zoning Applicable Law Certificate** language; Hamilton uses **Applicable Law Checklist** language; Mississauga directs applicants to check zoning and obtain any needed additional approvals before permit issuance. Because the labels differ by municipality, the province-wide product layer should say **zoning and applicable-law review/pre-check**, not hard-code one city’s service name across Ontario. citeturn24search11turn18view0turn10view5turn10view6turn17view1

**Electrical / ESA boundary wording**

Recommended wording:

> Electrical work is outside this package unless clearly identified as separate ESA documentation support. A building permit does not cover electrical work in Ontario, and most electrical work requires a separate ESA notification or permit and inspection process.

This is one of the clearest Ontario boundary lines and should appear prominently anywhere Vero lists package scope. citeturn10view7turn6search2turn6search16

**Occupancy / final inspection wording**

Recommended wording:

> Use “occupancy inspection,” “occupancy permit,” and “final inspection” only with a “where applicable” qualifier. Ontario municipalities do not use these end-stage terms in exactly the same way across all small residential workflows.

That recommendation is an inference from official municipal sources. Toronto emphasizes scheduling a **final inspection** to close the permit. Mississauga says a new home must pass the occupancy inspection done by building, plumbing, and HVAC inspectors before an **occupancy permit** can be obtained. Hamilton separately lists **Occupancy** and **Final – Completion of the Building Permit** and ties occupancy to an occupancy application form. Ottawa publicly refers to **occupancy permit** records. The safest Ontario-wide label is therefore **occupancy / final, where applicable**. citeturn18view3turn14view1turn15view1turn22search8

**External authority wording**

Recommended wording:

> Additional approvals outside the building permit review may still be required, depending on project type and location. These may include conservation authority approvals, heritage approvals, site plan approvals, highway-corridor approvals, sewage-system approvals, or other approvals that fall within Ontario “applicable law” or municipality-specific submission requirements.

That is exactly the kind of external-boundary wording Ontario-safe product copy should use. Provincial and municipal sources all make clear that building permits interact with broader applicable-law approvals and that some approvals must be in place before permit issuance. citeturn24search11turn24search4turn18view0turn10view6turn17view1turn20search3

## Ontario terms to use and BC or Vancouver terms to avoid

**Ontario authority terms to use**

Prefer the following terms in Ontario-facing copy:

- **building permit**
- **Application for a Permit to Construct or Demolish** when referring to the official provincial form
- **permit support package**
- **submission support package**
- **municipality**
- **municipal building department**
- **chief building official**
- **inspector** or **building inspector**
- **principal authority** for umbrella/legal-conservative copy
- **Schedule 1: Designer Information**
- **BCIN**
- **qualified designer**
- **architect**
- **professional engineer**
- **general review** only where actually applicable
- **zoning**
- **applicable law**
- **occupancy inspection / occupancy permit / final inspection, where applicable**
- **external approvals** or **other required approvals**

These are the terms Ontario’s own forms, provincial publications, regulator documents, and municipal pages actually use. citeturn21search0turn24search0turn25search0turn25search1turn18view0turn10view5turn23view1turn10view7

**BC/Vancouver terms to avoid**

Avoid the following as default Ontario wording:

- **Schedule C-B**
- **Letters of Assurance**
- **assurance package**
- **city-approved package**
- **code-certified package**
- **permit issuance package**
- **Vero compliance package**
- **AHJ-ready** as the primary customer-facing label
- **municipal sign-off by Vero**
- **professional assurance included**
- **occupancy sign-off** unless tied to a specific municipal process
- **final completion certificate** unless tied to an actual authority-issued document
- Vancouver-specific inspection-stage names or workflow assumptions carried into Ontario unchanged

The problem with these terms is not just style. Several of them imply a professional or municipal function that Ontario assigns elsewhere, or they import BC assurance language into a province that uses different legal forms and different regulatory actors. citeturn21search0turn25search0turn25search1turn23view1turn23view2

A practical nuance: **AHJ** can still exist as an internal shorthand, but Ontario’s official public sources are clearer and more user-comprehensible when they say **municipality**, **chief building official**, **inspector**, or **principal authority**. For Ontario customer-facing copy, Vero should generally prefer those official terms. citeturn24search0turn25search0turn25search1turn24search4

## Audience-specific wording variants

The three variants below all preserve the same core boundary: Vero supports document assembly and workflow organization, but the municipality or other principal authority reviews and decides, and required regulated professionals remain responsible for their own work. That is the correct Ontario posture. citeturn25search0turn25search1turn23view1turn10view7

**Builder-facing plain-English version**

> **Ontario Permit Support Package**  
> Draft only — review before use.  
> This package helps organize common documents for an Ontario building permit submission. It may include draft forms, drawings, document checklists, and placeholders for items your municipality or other authority may require.  
>  
> You are still responsible for confirming your municipality’s requirements, zoning and other applicable-law approvals, and any required designer or professional sign-off. Vero Permit does not issue permits, approve code compliance, replace municipal inspections, or replace your architect, engineer, BCIN designer, ESA filing, conservation authority approval, or other required authority process.

**Admin/internal version**

> **Ontario Authority Package — Internal Draft Label**

>  
> Required caution flags:  
> municipality-specific form set not confirmed; Schedule 1/BCIN requirement may apply; professional seal/general review may apply; zoning/applicable-law approvals may be outstanding; electrical may require separate ESA path; occupancy terminology varies by municipality; package is not production-approved without Ontario review.

**Authority-facing conservative version**

> **Ontario Building Permit Submission Support Package**  
> Draft document set assembled to support filing and review by the applicable municipality or other principal authority for the project identified herein.

## Risk notes, review requirements, and final recommended wording block

**Risk notes and review requirements**

This wording set is intentionally conservative, but it still requires review before release. First, Ontario’s code and amendment document continue to evolve, and the province has shifted to the 2024 code framework with ongoing amendments. Second, municipally exposed labels vary more than a BC-first product might expect. Third, some of the most regulated Ontario phrases—especially anything that names an official form, a Schedule 1 obligation, a general-review obligation, or an occupancy deliverable—should be checked against the current live municipality and current provincial form immediately before production deployment. citeturn19search13turn24search9turn17view1turn18view0turn10view5turn14view1turn15view1

The highest-risk overclaim areas for Vero are predictable: implying permit issuance, implying code compliance, implying zoning/applicable-law clearance, implying Vero-provided professional responsibility, implying ESA coverage, or implying a universal Ontario end-state document called an occupancy permit or final certificate. Those claims should remain hard-blocked in product copy unless Vero later creates tightly jurisdiction-specific overlays that have been reviewed against the live municipal process. citeturn24search4turn25search0turn25search1turn10view7turn18view3turn14view1turn22search3

A final process note: where Vero surfaces official Ontario form labels, it should preserve the exact official wording rather than rebranding it. For example, if the provincial form is attached, call it **Application for a Permit to Construct or Demolish** and call the schedule **Schedule 1: Designer Information**. This is safer than renaming regulated documents into platform-native marketing language. citeturn21search0turn19search14turn24search0

> **Ontario Permit Support Package**  
> *Draft only — requires review before use. Not production approved.*  
>  
> This permit support package has been assembled to help organize materials for an Ontario building permit submission for **[Project Address / Project Identifier]**. It is prepared for submission support only and is intended to assist the applicant in compiling project information, drawings, schedules, and supporting documents for review by **[Municipality / Principal Authority]**.  
>  
> This package does **not** issue a permit, does **not** determine Ontario Building Code compliance, does **not** confirm compliance with zoning or other applicable law, and does **not** replace review, acceptance, inspection, or decisions by the municipality, chief building official, inspector, or any other principal authority.  
>  
> Where required, the applicant must provide completed official forms and schedules, including **Application for a Permit to Construct or Demolish**, **Schedule 1: Designer Information**, and any municipality-specific submission materials. >  
> Additional approvals may be required before permit issuance or during review, including zoning and applicable-law approvals and, where applicable, conservation authority, heritage, highway, sewage-system, ESA electrical, or other external authority requirements.  
>  
> Inspection and closeout requirements vary by municipality and project type. References to occupancy inspection, occupancy permit, final inspection, or permit closeout apply **where applicable** and must be confirmed with **[Municipality / Principal Authority]**.

> **Ontario-only note:** Do not use BC Schedule C-B or other BC/Vancouver assurance language for this project package unless specifically required and reviewed for Ontario use.
