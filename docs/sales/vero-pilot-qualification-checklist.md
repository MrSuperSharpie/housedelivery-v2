# Vero Permit — Pilot Qualification Checklist

> Use this to decide whether a prospect is a good early pilot before investing time.
> Keep it honest. A bad pilot costs more than no pilot.
> Vero organizes inspection records and supports better documentation. It does not
> approve permits, certify compliance, or replace the city or any qualified professional.

---

## Who is a good pilot

- A BC builder or inspector who **already feels the pain** of scattered inspection
  records (photos in phones, notes in texts, reports in email).
- Running at least **one active project** we can use during the pilot window.
- Willing to give **honest, specific feedback** — including what doesn't work.
- Comfortable trying a new workflow without needing a big rollout.
- Values a **clean, organized handover** to owners or authorities.
- Reachable and responsive (a pilot needs a real point of contact).

## Who is a bad pilot

- Expects Vero to **approve permits, certify compliance, or speak for the city/AHJ.**
  (Re-set expectations or walk away — this will end badly.)
- Wants a guarantee of approval or faster permit outcomes.
- Has no active project to actually use it on.
- Won't give feedback or won't make time.
- Needs heavy customization or integrations before they'll try anything.
- Is shopping for a legal/code compliance decision tool.

---

## Required pilot conditions

- One named contact on the builder side **and**, where relevant, one inspector.
- At least one real project with upcoming inspection activity.
- Agreement on a simple **start and end date** for the pilot.
- Shared understanding, in writing, that:
  - Vero organizes records; it does not approve, certify, or decide compliance.
  - The city or proper authority retains final say.
- A quick mid-pilot and end-of-pilot feedback check-in scheduled up front.

---

## What project types to start with

- Single-family or small residential builds.
- Small multi-unit projects (a handful of units) with straightforward inspection flow.
- Projects where the builder already does inspections and just needs the records
  organized.
- Projects with a cooperative inspector or qualified professional involved.

## What project types to avoid (for now)

- Large, complex Part 3 buildings, towers, or institutional/commercial projects.
- Mixed-use or high-complexity projects with many disciplines and floors.
- Anything where the prospect expects Vero to drive a code/compliance outcome.
- Projects with no near-term inspection activity (nothing to demonstrate).
- High-stakes or contentious projects where a tooling experiment adds risk.

---

## What success looks like

- The pilot user keeps inspection records in Vero instead of scattered places.
- Photos, notes, deficiencies, reports, and field notes land in one project file.
- At least one clean, organized package or Field Note Record is produced and shared.
- The user says it **saved them time or hassle** versus their old workflow.
- The user would recommend it to another builder or inspector.

---

## What data to collect from the pilot

- How records were handled **before** vs. **with** Vero (qualitative).
- Number of inspections / field notes / reports captured during the pilot.
- Time saved (their estimate) on assembling or finding records.
- Friction points: where the workflow felt slow, confusing, or missing.
- Which features got used (evidence capture, Field Note Record, Vault package).
- A direct quote on what was most useful and what was missing.
- Whether they want to continue and on what terms.

---

## What NOT to promise

- That Vero will **approve a permit** or speed up permit timelines.
- That Vero **certifies compliance** or makes code/legal decisions.
- That Vero output is **accepted by the AHJ** or replaces an official submission.
- That records are a substitute for an inspector's or professional's judgment.
- Specific approval outcomes of any kind.

Promise only what's true: **Vero keeps inspection records organized in one secure
project file, and the city or proper authority retains final say.**
